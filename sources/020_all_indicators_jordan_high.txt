Do not use this indicator by itself to claim root cause. Pair it with local evidence such as attendance procedures, intervention records, student/family input, school climate evidence, and staff implementation evidence.

[INFO BOXES 3]
Summary: the school has one overall chronic absenteeism rate. Each subgroup row has its own rate. The Gap from Schoolwide column compares the subgroup rate with the schoolwide rate. A positive gap means the subgroup rate is higher than the schoolwide rate, which is a concern for chronic absenteeism. A negative gap means the subgroup rate is lower than the schoolwide rate, which may be a relative strength.

## WARNINGS

[WARNINGS 1]
Subgroup data limitation: the public CDE chronic absenteeism file reports single-category subgroup summaries. It can show groups such as Male, English Learners, and Students with Disabilities separately, but it does not provide intersectional counts such as Male English Learners with Disabilities. Subgroup counts should not be added together or interpreted as mutually exclusive.

[WARNINGS 2]
Interpretation guardrail: Absenteeism Reason data should be used to generate local follow-up questions, not to claim root cause. Public reason categories do not show why students were absent, whether attendance codes were applied consistently, or what intervention will work.

[WARNINGS 3]
Interpretive denominator caution: CAASPP reports assessment results beginning at 11 students with scores, but results based on 11-29 valid scores can change noticeably when only a few students change performance levels. For this ELA table, use extra caution with: Black or African American (n=19), Students with Disabilities (n=23). Treat these gaps as descriptive signals for local verification, not stable group characteristics.

[WARNINGS 4]

Treat these gaps as descriptive signals for local verification, not stable group characteristics.

[WARNINGS 5]
Dashboard color/status code note: California School Dashboard indicator files may include numeric status, change, and color code fields. In this local dashboard, these fields are shown only as reference codes from the source file. They should not be interpreted as literal chart colors unless verified against the official California School Dashboard rules for that indicator and year. For leadership inquiry, prioritize the actual rate, count, trend, system comparison, subgroup gap, and local evidence still needed.

[WARNINGS 6]

Use these data to generate local questions about discipline policy, incident categories, student supports, reentry, and due process; do not use them alone to claim root cause or evaluate individual staff members.

Los Angeles Unified School District (LAUSD) continues to employ a comprehensive set of tools and strategies to monitor and advance our implementation of the state academic standards adopted by the California State Board of Education. This ongoing work is guided by a commitment to continuous improvement and equity in student learning outcomes.  Interim Assessments: LAUSD actively utilizes the i-Ready Diagnostic Assessment in Reading and Math to measure student placement and growth in relation to grade-level standards. These assessments, aligned with the California academic standards, are administered three times a year—at the beginning, middle, and end—to inform instruction and support targeted intervention. Additionally, the District uses the Dynamic Indicators of Basic Early Literacy Skills (DIBELS) to assess foundational literacy skills in early grades, ensuring a strong start for our youngest learners.  State-Adopted Instructional Materials: LAUSD consistently adopts and implements instructional materials approved by the state that are aligned with the California Standards and curricular frameworks. These resources are used across all schools to deliver coherent instruction that builds sequentially from one grade level to the next, preparing students for college, career, and life success. Teachers continue to receive support in effectively utilizing these materials to deliver high-quality, standards-based instruction.  Teacher Observations and Feedback: Principals and instructional leaders regularly conduct classroom observations focused on the LAUSD Teaching and Learning Framework (TLF). Particular attention is given to Standard 3: Delivery of Instruction, Element C1: Standards-Based Projects, Activities, and Assignments. These observations enable ongoing dialogue and professional learning, ensuring teachers receive actionable feedback and support as they refine their instructional practices in alignment with state standards.   Student Performance Data: LAUSD continuously analyzes student performance data from multiple sources, including the Smarter Balanced Assessments, i-Ready, DIBELS, and other formative tools. This data is reviewed within the Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) cycle to inform decision-making at the school and district levels.
